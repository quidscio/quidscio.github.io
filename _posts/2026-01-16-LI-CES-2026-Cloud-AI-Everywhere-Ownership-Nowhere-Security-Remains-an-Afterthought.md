**AI is the real story**

Nearly everything at CES included AI, and almost none of it was locally inferenced. The state of the art in consumer products is cloud-connected. Pause for a moment - what does that mean?

> Vendors have chased subscription revenue for years. AI may be the final lever that ensures consumers rent rather than own products.

Cloud AI is expensive. Vendors have chased subscription revenue for years. AI may be the final lever that ensures consumers rent rather than own products. Cost, operational stability, and availability all become contingent.

Cloud computing and AI also expand attack surface dramatically. Where is your data? Who has it? Is it shared, sold, or stolen? What about prompt poisoning? Personal data now flows through more third parties than ever, and cloud subscription services naturally invite advertising models.

Supply chains become more complex and less transparent overnight. Most engineers I spoke with, particularly at smaller companies, are not considering supply chain risk at all. More exposure with less planning leads to more breach notices:

> “We’re sorry. There’s no evidence your data has been used yet. We’ll do better. Here’s a year of credit monitoring.”

Sounds grim.

**Here is the silver lining**

For anyone focused on supply chain protection, opportunity is everywhere. Organizations need pragmatic guidance. Most begin with SSDF, NIST 800-218. I recommend starting with a unified approach using Proactive-SSCRM to address the problem more completely, roughly 42 percent more risk awareness (coverage) than SSDF.

For a fast on-ramp, see *Closing the Chain: How to Reduce Your Risk of Being SolarWinds, Log4j, or XZ Utils* [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.12192](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.12192). This paper uses real incidents to define a practical “starter kit” of tasks. Better yet, tailor that starter kit to the threats most relevant to your organization.
